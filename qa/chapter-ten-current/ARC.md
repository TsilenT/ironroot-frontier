# Chapter 10: The Last Ascent — proposed five-encounter arc

Design source, not production mission definitions. Sequential prerequisites below
refer to this proposed arc; admission awaits canonical Chapter 9 completion and
independent balance, save, story and ending review. No optional health medal or
artificial near-death outcome gates the story. Damaged victories count.

Source status: the first encounter now has a private factory/evaluator and
focused tests in `foothold.mjs` / `foothold.test.mjs`, using registered native
mechanics. Its private opening revision 2 includes one rearward foundry pacing
correction; a paid clear and actual-player difficulty remain unestablished.
The finale has the separately tested colossus integration lab. The three middle
encounters below remain proposals, not placeholder campaign entries.

| Encounter | Proposed battlefield / objective | Decision and pressure | Story role / prerequisite | Unproven limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Below the Ramparts | Classic, clear; destroy two of three original exposed outposts, or enemy core | Wounded rear camp versus visible supported forward production. Cover threatened roads while choosing which outposts to break; accept an exposed building loss to fund a breakthrough. | Establish a foothold after Chapter 9, conditional on its eventual canonical release. | Two distinct affordable openings and a recovery window need testing. Preplaced active siege is unnecessary; ordinary factories supply pressure. |
| The Last Granary | Long March, clear; destroy two original rear mines, or enemy core | Longer reinforcement travel makes economy raids costly. Mobile supported pressure versus a slower screened siege line; side pressure keeps the raid from being free. | Cut real funding after establishing the foothold. | Destroyed mines must actually remove purchase income. Existing factories still spawn without wages; no claim that raids stop all waves. Original-target identity and alternate core route need strict admission tests. |
| No Fires Tonight | Classic, fog; break a charted outer command post, or enemy core | Known fort location, unknown moving troops and later construction. Advance enough to scout, or spend on overlapping defensive coverage before committing. | Find the ascent through the dark after the supply raid. | Use a genuinely registered fog identity with ordinary autonomous air. No hidden deadline, unseen teleport, or objective leak. Fog/contact readability and viable scouting remain untested. |
| Carry the Crossing | Twin Span, clear; hold both crossings for cumulative control time, or enemy core | Split a finite army between two roads, or concentrate and accept lost control time. Opponent production sustains counterattacks while reinforcements travel. | Bring the company and supplies through the final crossing after the fog approach. | Requires a new explicitly registered two-road authored control contract; current campaign control is Classic-only. Neither marked map art nor a skirmish identity alone implements this mission. Hold duration and cumulative-loss rules remain proposals. |
| The Last Engine | Classic, clear; enemy core and its one-time colossus both destroyed while own core survives | Visible entrenched production and a disclosed last weapon at 600 enemy-core HP. Keep rear support/income or concentrate for a fast assault and rebuild after the local breakout. All roads can strike the physical body; the damaged emergence creates an opportunity, not invulnerability. | Final confrontation after the crossing. Show the weapon and exact breakout terms before Begin, then a single deployment cue. | Current source lab verifies mechanics only. No player-tested difficulty, rendered disclosure, production save identity or ending integration. Do not invalidate an efficient victory because it finishes comfortably. |

The five encounters teach different decisions: establish territory, remove actual
funding, acquire information, divide control, and recover after a disclosed local
reversal. Only the finale has a boss. Map variety is intentional but requires
real supported objective contracts rather than cosmetic map changes. These are
five Chapter 10 encounters; this document makes no ten-chapter completion claim.
